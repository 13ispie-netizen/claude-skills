# Adapt Cold Email

Erin's cold email asking a stranger for a 25-minute Zoom. Five beats, always in this order. Short, warm, a little playful, never stiff.

## Intake (one message, then go)

Need: **person's name**, **org**, and either **a hook from Erin** or **a link** (bio, LinkedIn, org page). If Erin gave all three, skip asking.

## Research (keep it lean: 2-4 calls max)

1. Fetch the link Erin gave. Pull: the person's exact title, the org's own buzzwords, one specific thing the person or org said/made/did, and any constraint (not accepting proposals, sunsetting, closed cycle, geography).
2. **Verify the role.** If the page doesn't name the person, run one web search for "[name] [org]". If Erin's title is wrong (e.g. "program officer" vs Director), say so in chat.
3. **Warm-path check:** one Gmail search for the org's domain and the person's name. If anyone at that org already knows Erin, flag it in chat. A mutual connection beats any cold hook. Skip if Gmail is unavailable.
4. Do not read further pages unless the hook is still missing.

Prefer a hook in the person's own words. If only org-level material exists, use it and tell Erin in chat which source it came from, and ask whether she's actually seen/heard it.

## The template

**Subject:** the ask, as a short question in their terms. Ex: "Still time to partner with AmbitioUS?"

```
Hi [First name],

[1. HOOK: 2-4 sentences. One specific thing they said/made, Erin's honest reaction, and "I HAVE to meet you / the team behind this." Playful, a little self-deprecating is welcome.]

Could we connect over Zoom for 25 minutes in the next couple of weeks?

[3. MISSION, MIRRORED] A little about me: I am the Executive Director of [Architecture + Advocacy](https://architectureandadvocacy.org), a youth-led nonprofit in South LA and Flatbush, Brooklyn. We work at the intersection of community organizing and architecture (hello, [their one key word]!!) so communities of color have real [their word, e.g. self-determination / a say] over what gets built in their neighborhoods. [Their sector's entry point, e.g. "Art and creativity"] is our entry point to civic power: residents imagine the future of their own blocks, and then we build it with them.

When a community group owns the land, we help them build on the spaces they already have. When they don't (yet!), we work with the architect/developer to make sure residents still shape what gets built.

[4. THEIR CONSTRAINT, ASKED STRAIGHT: one or two sentences naming the real obstacle and asking directly. Ex: "I know AmbitioUS is set to sunset in 2028-- is there still time to partner with you all?"]

You can book a time that works for your schedule here: https://calendar.app.google/w1SbkYs4j4b8Q42H7

If none of those work, let me know and we will figure out something that does.

-E
```

## Rules

- **Ask is plain text, not bold.**
- **Title, not "I run":** "I am the Executive Director of."
- **A+A is "youth-led."** Never "community-led design nonprofit" here.
- **Translate the entry point into their field,** not just their buzzwords: arts funder → "art and creativity"; planning/land funder → "design"; civic/democracy funder → "civic engagement".
- **Mirror lightly.** Echo one or two of their words, in the "(hello, ___!!)" slot and the self-determination slot. Never stack their jargon.
- **The two-sides paragraph stays** in every email, worded as above.
- **Beat 4 is direct.** Name the constraint and ask the real question. No "pick your brain" softening, no flattery about their network. If there's no constraint, use one sentence on why *them specifically* (overlap in place, population, or model), then stop.
- **Sign "-E"** and include Erin's Gmail signature underneath.
- No em dashes (`--` only if needed). No hedging or caveats in the email itself; flags go in chat.
- Under ~200 words in the body.

## Output

1. Show the draft in chat with 1-3 short flags (role mismatch, hook source, warm path).
2. When Erin says to draft it, create a Gmail draft: `to` = their address if found (else blank and say so), subject per above, HTML body with inline links, `includeSignature: true`. If Gmail isn't connected, give the text ready to paste.
3. Erin usually schedules early-morning sends herself; don't send.
